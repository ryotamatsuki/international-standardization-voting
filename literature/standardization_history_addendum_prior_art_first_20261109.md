## 3. Closest theorem screen

- **Gandal & Shy (2001):** governments' recognition of foreign standards, national domestic-consumer benefits and home firms' commercial interests, regional standards union. Source: https://doi.org/10.1016/S0022-1996(00)00067-2 . Formal ISO technical text approval is different from G&S foreign recognition, not inherently an original economic mechanism.
- **Barrett & Yang (2001):** domestic installed base makes firms and governments rationally preserve incompatible technologies. Working-paper proposition mapping already exists in the repo; installed-base reluctance is NOT independent novelty.
- **Klimenko (2009):** network strength and national strategic compatibility policy induce excess or inadequate compatibility; stronger networks inducing restraint is NOT obviously new.
- **Goerke & Holler (1995) and Spulber (2019):** voting and market welfare; Spulber Economic Journal Proposition 6 explicitly provides conditions for efficient standard voting plus competitive outcomes. DOI https://doi.org/10.1111/ecoj.12606 . Mere inefficiency by changed 3/4 quota is NOT novelty.
- **Zhao-Xia-Shaw (2007):** developers, passive adopters, nonadopters endogenous simultaneously; publishing/adopting two distinct decisions is NOT novel by itself.
- **Boom (2001), Ruiz (2004), Grossman-McCalman-Staiger (2021):** endogenous firm attributes under standard/regulatory institutions plus country or consumer welfare. JPET chain-rule split D - L and the existence of private product-design spillovers are NOT a new general methodology.
- **Buccella-Fanti-Gori (2023), Cheng-Huang (2025):** product compatibility and veto under network effects. Exact published theorem exclusion remains incompletely checked where inaccessible.
- **Bertoletti (2026):** market coverage can change normative compatibility outcomes: the repo's full-coverage welfare results must not be generalized.
- **Farrell-Simcoe (2012), Simcoe (2012), Belleflamme (2002):** bargaining delays, technical revisions, committee/de facto competition; naive approval-revision results likely absorbed.
- **Blind-von Laer (2022), Mattli-Büthe (2003), Chen-Xiao (2026):** national leverage and standards participation are empirically studied; participation itself is not a novel phenomenon.

## 4. ISO as an actual institution, not an invented binary public policy

2026 ISO/IEC Directives Part 1, section 2.7, https://assets.iec.ch/sd/ISO-IEC_Directives_Part-1_2026.html :
- DIS/FDIS is a nation-body vote, not a national government's unconstrained welfare-maximizing choice; national body aggregates domestic stakeholders and comments should be technical.
- Dual threshold: at least 2/3 approval from voting committee P-members and no more than 1/4 negative of all voting national members, abstentions excluded.
- DIS may go directly to publication if no technical changes, FDIS not always held.
- Failure sends proposal to committee for revision; it need not be permanent fragmentation.
- Published standard does not compel firms to implement it; ISO/IEC Guide 21-1 distinguishes national adoption from firm-level use.
- The repo's four all-valid P members supporting at least 3/4 is an exact narrow simplification ONLY if second threshold all-member vote condition also passes.
- A country with a negative ISO ballot need not prevent voluntary domestic or foreign implementation. Do not infer national compliance directly from ballot.

## 5. Null-model exclusions before suggesting any theorem

A1: Holding firms' feasible designs, costs, information and expectation selection unchanged, ISO publication itself cannot alter the unique continuation equilibrium. This is already written in the repo as KS-C06, and is a null check, NOT a new theorem.

A2: A one-country-one-vote quota acting on arbitrary exogenous national welfare numbers merely creates order-statistics and majority winners, the content of which Goerke-Holler and standard voting theory already explain. Quota arithmetic is not a new mechanism.

A3: Holding national vote and technical scope fixed, private installation/legacy lock-in and failed adoption are classical Barrett-Yang/Zhao outcomes.

A4: Holding firm adoption/design fixed, a regulation can change national consumer and profit interests (G&S/Costinot/Klimenko). Direct voting effects are not new.

A5: Holding standard scope and firms constant, revision costs/delays already exist in Farrell-Simcoe/Belleflamme.

A6: A formal standard can leave vendor products not actually interoperable; real-world observation is documented. Needs a new predictive comparative static not described by earlier implementation literature.

## 6. Interim scientific judgment

**No independently vetted new economic theorem (N3/N4) is established in this pass.** Do not rank hypothetical detailed algebra as contributions. A meaningful surviving FRONTIER, not a certified proposition, is whether *endogenous committee choice of technical scope* and *strategic post-publication design/voluntary implementation* jointly produce an observable discrepancy between formal harmonization and realized international interoperability/trade. The novelty has to come from a demonstrable nonadditive interaction that cannot be derived from G&S, Spulber, Boom, Zhao, GMS, Klimenko, not merely combining these game stages.

Require a fully specified minimal-game theorem whose qualitative prediction disappears in at least two economic ablations: exogenous post-publication design/implementation; exogenous technical scope; and/or removal of national aggregation. If it survives any one null model unchanged, it is probably a familiar mechanism given new labels.

Any candidate with a long list of knife-edge national weights, special costs borne by a minority despite free opt-out, special designated proposer or impossible ISO vote must be marked weak. A theorem must explain commercially important outcomes (implementation, conformance, cross-border trade, access, migration and market coverage) and allow independently measurable exposure, not merely a complicated quartic root.
