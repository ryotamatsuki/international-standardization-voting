## Historical spine, findings which must NOT be advertised as original
- 1985–1988: Katz–Shapiro (1985,1986), Farrell–Saloner (1985,1986,1988): network adoption, installed base, momentum/lock-in, standard-setting committees versus markets.
- 1990s: Weiss–Sirbu (1990), Besen–Farrell (1994), Axelrod et al (1995), Goerke–Holler (1995), Economides–Flyer (1998): actual committee technology choice, standards coalitions, voting and welfare, asymmetric compatible subsets.
- 2001–09: Gandal–Shy (2001) foreign-regulation recognition and national firm/world profit; Barrett–Yang (2001) rational deviations from international standards; Boom (2001) post-policy endogenous product specifications; Ruiz (2004 WP) government foreign standard acceptance followed by endogenous product attributes; Zhao–Xia–Shaw (2007) consortia developer/passive adopter/nonadopter stages; Costinot (2008) mutual recognition versus national treatment; Klimenko (2009) strategic national under/over-compatibility policies. These are not the same game, but they threaten every isolated generic mechanism.
- 2012–19: Farrell–Simcoe (2012) rule design and delay; Simcoe (2012) IETF committee delay and data; Spulber (2019) explicit voting plus market competition, Proposition 6 gives efficiency under assumptions.
- 2021–25: Grossman–McCalman–Staiger (2021) private product design and international regulatory agreements; Schmidt–Steingress (2022) standardization and trade; Buccella–Fanti–Gori (2023) voluntary private compatibility and SPNE; Cheng–Huang (2025) quality and private compatibility veto.

## Economic frontier, NOT a certified novelty assertion
The boundaries between publicly approved technical text, national transposition, vendor conformance/optional feature support, actual cross-vendor interoperability, and imports/market entry may generate independent predictions. But standards development+adoption already in Zhao et al 2007, voting+market in Spulber 2019, endogenous redesign in Boom/Ruiz/GMS, and implementation/market coverage in new 2026 papers. Any result must turn on a NEW interaction and be compared with these originals.
