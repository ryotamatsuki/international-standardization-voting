# H5 prior-art falsification: published results, 1998–2026
Date: 2026-11-09 JST. STATUS: verified overlapping literatures; **not** a certificate of originality. Source classification: published article [P], working paper [WP], primary official report [O], separate conjecture/model [M].

## Executive finding
The previous highest-priority H5 formulation—technical-text harmonization may not deliver market access unless conformity assessment is mutually recognized—is **NOT NEW**. Nor is national capture of residual non-tariff barriers or the discreteness of certification costs. This note reclassifies the old H5 headline **NO-GO**.

### Direct overlaps, in chronological order
1. **Wallner (1998), WP 254, "Mutual Recognition and the Strategic Use of International Standards"** [WP original PDF accessible at https://swopec.hhs.se/hastef/papers/hastef0254.pdf]. Abstract and introduction pp.1–3 explicitly model foreign-supplier cost increases from domestic standards as a strategic tool for domestic welfare, including a three-country Cournot triopoly where large countries can recognize each other's standards at the expense of a smaller country. The introduction also discusses the pre-existing *law of constant protection*, i.e., substitution between tariff and non-tariff instruments. Model concerns recognition of product standards, NOT the acceptance of foreign conformity-assessment test reports.
2. **Costinot (2008), JIE 75:197–213, published full PDF https://economics.mit.edu/sites/default/files/publications/standards_final.pdf** [P]. NT vs MR under incomplete agreements, quality and technical compatibility standards, unilateral strategic regulatory choices. Its Lemma 1 and regime comparisons do not include a separate endogenous decision on recognition of test reports. Nevertheless national regulatory protection and welfare-regime reversal are established, not new.
3. **An & Maskus (2009), The World Economy 32(4):552–574, DOI https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-9701.2008.01150.x** [P abstract directly verified]. Firm-level survey compares *international product standard alignment* and *mutual-recognition agreements*; only MRAs significantly predicted exports in their data, consistent with stronger effects on fixed export costs. **This already states the earlier H5 comparative claim.** Not a randomized causal policy estimate and not a formal endogenous-retaliation theorem.
4. **OECD (2016), von Lampe, Deconinck & Bastien, Trade Policy Paper 195, https://doi.org/10.1787/3fbf60b1-en** [O, original PDF]. Treats specification costs, conformity assessment costs and information costs separately. pp.42–49 (PDF page locations vary) explicitly model importer-regulator choice between SDoC, recognition of foreign tests/certificates, and domestic-only CAP, trading export costs against confidence and expected damage of noncompliance; notes incentives, exporter moral hazard, sanctions, repeated relationships. Thus **regulator choice among modes** and **confidence-cost tradeoff** are old.
5. **McDaniels & Karttunen (2016), WTO Working Paper ERSD-2016-09, https://www.wto.org/english/res_e/reser_e/ersd201609_e.htm** [WP]. WTO concerns show duplicate testing, delay and discrimination are trade barriers. No novel description of residual testing barrier.
6. **Schmidt & Steingress (2022), JIE, DOI https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jinteco.2022.103619** [P]. Endogenous firm standards adoption, fixed costs, extensive/import intensive margins. A mere switch of fixed cost channel is not a new analytical contribution.
7. **Maggi & Mrázová (2024 WP33318), https://www.nber.org/papers/w33318** [WP, not verified peer-reviewed status]. Original downloadable text, especially footnote 6 in the Introduction, says certification costs can change *discontinuously* if national standards differ even slightly and require repeated certification; §3.4 **Proposition 5** states that, in THEIR one-way-trade cooperative versus noncooperative comparison, international agreement never reduces trade at the extensive margin. That conclusion has **different policy experiment and assumptions** than an exogenous harmonization shock with endogenous binary CAP selection. Do not mischaracterize it as a contradicted universal theorem.
8. **OECD (2026-11-05), Trade Policy Paper 307, https://doi.org/10.1787/56f2cfc7-en** [O]. Explains norm recognition vs institution/CAB recognition, even aligned standards may lack accepting foreign certifications; reinforces importance, **not novelty**.
9. **WTO TBT Agreement Articles 5.1.1/5.1.2 and 6, https://www.wto.org/english/tratop_e/tbt_e/tbtagr.htm** [O]. CAP procedures may not unfairly disadvantage foreign suppliers or create unnecessary obstacles, and mutual recognition is subject to sufficient confidence. Any proposed domestic retesting policy must have defensible verification/confidence differences and proportionate costs. The basic positive model does NOT prove WTO legality.
10. **METI, government vs accreditation-body MRA distinction, https://www.meti.go.jp/policy/economy/hyojun-kijun/mr/mr.html** [O]. Accreditation-body equivalence does not automatically bind governments to accept foreign product certificates.

### An additional 2026 research collision
**Bourreau, Raizonville & Thébaudin (2026), Journal of Industrial Economics 74(2):197–215, DOI 10.1111/joie.70018** [P] jointly solve platform interoperability and endogenous consumer multi-homing; large installed base can make mandate inefficient. This lowers generic H9 novelty as well. **Berends, Deken & Hummel (2026), Research Policy 55(10), article 105609** [P empirical] document combination of open-third-party and proprietary platform modes; multi-standard coexistence itself is observed, not a new theorem.

## What is newly proved IN THE NEW MINI-MODEL, not in the wider literature
- P0: a **no-over-offset lemma** for an optimal *continuous additive* conformity burden, provided strictly convex administrative cost and no direct harmonization effect except through net entry cost. Known monotone-comparative-statics logic, N1.
- P1: a **binary-regime switch theorem** with a unique threshold: a harmonization shock can cause a discontinuous change from accepting foreign conformity assessments to mandatory domestic retesting and reduce exporter entry locally. Formally proved within the new micro-model and verified symbolically. Mathematical result is not evidence of a general N3 economic mechanism. Directly depends on **genuinely discrete, inflexible institutional menu** and on a concave national objective in entrants; latter needs product-market foundation. Related switching, certification and strategic non-tariff protection literature are substantial, so N2 maximum pending exact closest-paper comparison.

## Critical non-containment tests before claim of international-paper originality
1. Does one of Wallner, Maggi–Mrázová, Costinot or the full conformity-policy literature explicitly characterize the *same* switch-induced extensive-margin decline? Original full comparisons partly still open.
2. Can concavity of the importing-country objective in imported exporter mass arise from a solved oligopoly or heterogeneous firm equilibrium without adding political weights to engineer the switch?
3. Is binary non-recognition of ALL foreign reports legally/technically justified by objectively different assurance, or prohibited by WTO TBT? Could a legal equivalent system allow continuous adjustments, in which case P1 disappears?
4. Does the sharp threshold persist with differentiated exporters, third-country effects, entry spillovers, and exporter-specific certification? Policy change must affect observed trade, not mere nominal publication.
5. Do discrete certification-program changes occur *because* standards harmonize in real industries rather than independently due to safety failures and accession negotiations?

**Editorial:** Original H5 standalone NO-GO. New P1 is a verified **conditional within-model theorem** worth a narrow institutional screening exercise: RESEARCH / N2 max. No N3 or independent publishable claim established.
