# Economics-first international-standardization frontier: literature and institutions
Date: 2026-11-09 JST. Research audit, not a systematic review with proof of literature absence. No new formal theorem claimed.

## 1. Separate the economic objects
(A) technical text approved by SSO, (B) national transposition, (C) product implementation/conformance, (D) cross-product functional interoperability, (E) mutual recognition of conformity-assessment results, (F) market access, trade, entry and welfare. These are neither synonyms nor stages with deterministic transitions.
Government recognition of *foreign standards* in Gandal–Shy 2001 is distinct from technical-selection votes of ISO national bodies, from importing-market acceptance of a foreign *test report*, and from software interoperability. ISO and WTO distinguish voluntary standards from mandatory technical regulations.

## 2. What the published theory already explains (1985–2021)
- Katz–Shapiro (1985, AER), Farrell–Saloner (1985, RAND; 1986, AER), Katz–Shapiro (1986, JPE): network externality, coordination failures, installed bases, excess inertia/momentum. These are **not new mechanisms**.
- Farrell–Saloner (1988, RAND): committees, delay, market coordination. Besen–Farrell (1994, JEP), Axelrod et al. (1995, Management Science), Economides–Flyer (1998): standards/compatibility alliances and competition within versus for markets.
- Goerke–Holler (1995, Public Choice): voting majority rule, network benefit vs variety and welfare. General majority inefficiency is not new.
- Gandal–Shy (2001, JIE): governmental standard recognition; network effect and conversion cost; mutually exclusive regional standardization clubs. Barrett–Yang (2001, JIE; accessible 1999 WP propositions 1/3/4/6/7): incumbent installed base, strategic divergence from international standards, domestic government non-enforcement. Both directly threaten voting-reversal narratives.
- Boom (2001, *Journal of Industrial Economics*, *On the Desirability of Compatibility with Product Selection*), compatibility and downstream product choice; Zhao–Xia–Shaw (2007, JMIS), endogenous developer/passive-adopter/nonadopter statuses; Costinot (2008, JIE), rules of product-standard agreements; Klimenko (2009, JIE), non-cooperative national compatibility and excess/insufficient interoperability. No novelty from chaining their game stages.
- Farrell–Simcoe (2012, RAND), Simcoe (2012, AER): rules, bargaining, committees and delay. Spulber (2019, Economic Journal), Proposition 6: in his no-SEP competitive adopted-groups framework majority votes select the efficient standard, notwithstanding market-mediated interests. Adding an SSO ballot then market pricing is not new. His theorem is **conditional**, not a universal efficiency theorem.
- Grossman–McCalman–Staiger (2021, Econometrica): regulatory cooperation and endogenous firm responses. Distinguish from the rejected JPET manuscript (three-country Cournot with endogenous residual design) and from this repository's four-country Salop–Bertrand/ISO models; no hybrid result asserted.

## 3. The 2022–2026 change in what can be predicted/observed
- Schmidt–Steingress (2022, JIE): standard harmonization raises exports and new entry in heterogeneous-firm data/model; harmonization => market access is not unexplored. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jinteco.2022.103619
- Buccella–Fanti–Gori (2023 and online 2025 / issue 2026), Cheng–Huang (2025, Journal of Economics): compatibility choice/costs/firm veto and strong network effects; shape of compatibility response not prima facie new. https://doi.org/10.1007/s00712-024-00896-5
- Berends–Deken–Hummel (2026, Research Policy, doi:10.1016/j.respol.2026.105609): observed 97 smart-home platforms increasingly combine third-party protocols and in-house interfaces, rather than choose exactly one. Empirical propositions on platform modes; NOT a game-theory proof of welfare optimality.
- Rosero Abad–Bekkers–Raiteri (2026-07-01, Journal of Standardisation): four case studies identify complement supply incentives as a condition for successful open standards. https://doi.org/10.59490/jos.2026.8038
- Ott–Roter–Krämer–Ackermann (2026-07-24, Journal of Competition Law & Economics): strategic dilution and delay of interoperability specification by incumbents under EU compulsory interoperability governance. A bigger specification may hurt implementation is ALREADY an identified qualitative mechanism; full article checked. https://doi.org/10.1093/joclec/nhag023
- Comino–Fedele–Manenti (2026-08-12, Journal of Industrial Economics): cybersecurity investment and interoperability with endogenous hacker incentives; security externality alone not new. https://doi.org/10.1111/joie.70041
- Bergeaud–Schmidt–Zago (2026-06, Journal of Financial Economics): patent proximity to standards forecasts initial firm sales/share and R&D, with subsequent catch-up. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jfineco.2026.104281
- Chen–Xiao (2026-04-27, JIBS): techno-nationalist disclosure restrictions can diminish home-firm standards influence; empirical event study, not a solved international voting theorem. https://doi.org/10.1057/s41267-026-00856-9
- Bianchi–Martimort–Straub (2024 WP revised 2026-10, TSE): digital-payments interoperability raises transactions but may soften quality/fee competition; endogenous coverage and welfare ambiguity. Not established as a peer-reviewed article. https://www.tse-fr.eu/publications/working-paper/does-interoperability-promote-competition-theory-and-evidence-digital
- Lessmann–Yao (2026-08 CESifo WP12923): ISO access/export capacity and certification correlate with trade; working paper, not peer-reviewed. https://www.ifo.de/en/cesifo/publications/2026/working-paper/access-standards-access-markets-iso-access-quality-infrastructure
- OECD (2026-11-05, Trade Policy Paper 307): harmonized technical norms alone often do not secure international acceptance of test/certification results or an effective customs workflow. A **policy evidence frontier**, not a mathematical novelty certificate. https://doi.org/10.1787/56f2cfc7-en
- Rocha–Thakur (2026, Canadian Journal of Economics): trade relations transmit regulation adoption. https://doi.org/10.1111/caje.70049
- Yang (2024, Journal of Economic Surveys): broad standards review. https://ideas.repec.org/a/bla/jecsur/v38y2024i3p717-758.html

## 4. What changes in the research frontier
The relevant outcome can now be disaggregated: document harmonization, product certification, international certificate acceptance, genuine vendor-to-vendor interoperability, exporting and price/quality. This is a useful **research agenda**, but every pairwise gap has known antecedents. The economic contribution, if any, must come from a jointly necessary strategic choice with an independently testable comparative static.

## 6. Access/evidence caveats
Direct full HTML: Spulber 2019, Ott et al. 2026, Berends et al. 2026, ISO and OECD institutional pages; early published publisher abstracts or accessible WP: Gandal–Shy, Barrett–Yang, Klimenko, Cheng–Huang, several others. One accessible version does NOT establish exact equivalence to a closed published proof. Working papers and empirical studies must not be presented as published abstract theory theorems.
