## Mathematical vs economic novelty
The exact scope–adoption peak derived in this gate is a **new formula within the proposed reduced-form game**, not a proof of a previously unknown general economic mechanism. The derivative of s*a(s)^2 is an elasticity comparison and follows directly from voluntary compliance with increasing implementation cost. It does not require ISO voting, post-standard Cournot/Salop product-market competition or network effects (theta may equal zero), so MUST NOT be described as a new ISO/network theorem. Its strength is clarity and a measurable optional-profile outcome, not certified N3.

## Newly examined exact 2026 adversarial-standards collision
**Ott, Roter, Krämer & Ackermann (2026), Journal of Competition Law & Economics**, https://doi.org/10.1093/joclec/nhag023 , **publisher full HTML directly read**. It describes deliberate "standard dilution": incumbents may add technically hard but not especially procompetitive features, making standards slow/ineffective; they may also withhold unique technical information. A fallback official technical-specification route may discipline the behavior. Thus the broad qualitative phenomenon "overly complex features reduce usefulness/implementation" was articulated in real-world standards before this research note. This prior art **further disqualifies the optional-profile D1 hump from a high-novelty economic headline**, even though Ott et al. are not an equivalent fully solved voluntary-adoption calculus. Ott's scenario is **mandatory** interoperability under EU regulation, whereas OCA optional certification is **voluntary**: do not conflate legal settings. To claim N3, prove a genuinely new strategic committee/implementation interaction with distinct refutable implications, not the phenomenon itself.
