# P1-KS four-paper theorem-level prior-art crosswalk (2026-11-09)

**Scope and epistemic status.** This is a close comparison of verifiably accessible portions of the relevant original publications and public author versions. It does **not** establish exhaustive formal non-inclusion in inaccessible published proofs. A different game form or different equation is **not sufficient** for economic novelty. Claims that follow solely from general welfare accounting, installed-base advantage, or majority vote arithmetic should be classified as old or low novelty pending more evidence.

## 1. Barrett & Yang (2001), *Journal of International Economics* 54:171–191

Published article: https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0022199600000829
DOI: https://doi.org/10.1016/S0022-1996(00)00082-9
Directly examined full prepublication **revised August 1999** IATRC working paper #99-9: https://ageconsearch.umn.edu/record/14597/files/wp9909.pdf

- WP Proposition 1 (roughly p.14): with an installed base of domestic technology, the incumbent's sales are increasing in compatibility with its domestic legacy technology, favoring deviation from an external international standard.
- WP Proposition 3 (roughly p.16): full voluntary adoption of the international standard requires no competing existing domestic technology or perfect old/new compatibility; otherwise domestic firms shift technology toward the domestic legacy standard.
- WP Proposition 4 (roughly p.18): larger established domestic base widens incumbent price markup over imported-standard rivals and raises exclusion prospects, subject to restrictions openly acknowledged in its appendix.
- WP Proposition 6 (roughly p.24): domestic-welfare government has no incentive to mandate international compliance when domestic old-technology consumers exist.
- WP Proposition 7 (roughly p.24): in mature network products, government may maximize domestic consumer surplus by enforcing the old technology.
- Their mechanisms additionally operate through inherited user switching costs, redesign and preexisting national technologies, under quantity/technology competition with expected network effects.
- **P1-KS overlap:** common historical installed base h, gain from maintaining old interoperability, government opposition, private-vs-national incentives. Strong overlap. The generic sign \(\partial_h\beta^*<0\) is therefore NOT a safe stand-alone novelty claim.
- **Structural distinction:** P1-KS models four firms with a three-plus-one interoperable legacy group, a current-user FE Salop price game, voluntary adoption after publication and **K national equal voting rights** with configurable q and profit-ownership matrix. The working paper does **not** derive that exact committee-quota support order-statistic construction. But this absence from the examined WP does not constitute proof of novelty.
- **Caution:** proposition numbers above are for the 1999 WP only. **Do not attribute the same numbering to the inaccessible final 2001 version without checking it.**

## 2. Klimenko (2009), *Journal of International Economics* 77:151–166

Publisher page with extended Introduction and section summaries:
https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0022199608001244
DOI: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jinteco.2008.08.005
Author institutional entry: https://econ.gatech.edu/publications/pub/1491

- Considers home/foreign differentiated products with incompatible networks, national policies aimed at technical compatibility, both perfect competition and international duopoly. Governments cannot rely on conventional trade instruments.
- Its **published extended introduction** explicitly states that depending on network-effect strength, noncooperative government policies can induce excessively high compatibility and trade or too little compatibility/trade, relative to worldwide coordination.
- The introduction of the published paper says it distinguishes foreign competition benefits to domestic users, network integrity of domestic users, strategic rent-shifting, price competition distortions, and bilateral/FTA compatible standards.
- **P1-KS overlap:** strong network effects alter government compatibility decisions and can be inefficient. **High direct novelty threat** to current voting-reversal story.
- **Distinction from available text:** Klimenko analyzes national regulatory compatibility policies and coordination in two-country settings, not a K-body yes/no approval committee with voluntary adoption cost after publication, mixed ownership and an arbitrary q quota. The last four objects nevertheless could simply be a reduced-form committee wrapper around already-known payoffs, so difference is not self-justifying.
- **Limit:** full published main-theorem equations/proofs were not publicly accessible. Therefore formal theorem-by-theorem noncontainment is **UNRESOLVED**, and new claims must not be described as clear novelty.

## 3. Cheng & Huang (2025), *Journal of Economics* 145:31–57

Published DOI: https://doi.org/10.1007/s00712-024-00896-5
Publisher readable abstract, notes and Appendix 1: https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00712-024-00896-5

- Models vertical quality differentiation, a quality-choice stage, product compatibility, network effects and bilateral firm veto.
- Abstract result: when both firms have veto power, strong network externalities make compatibility unattainable; without veto power, compatibility emerges.
- Public Note 10 and Appendix 1 show asymmetric firm-level profit incidence may occur, including \(\pi_1^c<\pi_1^n\), while firm2 benefits; thus "incumbent loses while entrant gains" is NOT prima facie new.
- **P1-KS overlap:** veto-driven incompatibility under stronger network effects; asymmetric firm gains; compatibility/quality strategic decision.
- **Structural distinction:** published accessible text does not introduce K national standard-setting bodies with domestic-CS + world-profit national voting or national population-share ownership thresholds. Current P1-KS does not have endogenous quality choice.
- **Limit:** full published proof text is subscription preview; strong exact noncontainment verdict cannot be issued.

## 4. Bertoletti (2026, in press), *Review of Industrial Organization*

2025 accessible university working paper 560, complete text:
https://repec.dems.unimib.it/repec/pdf/mibwpaper560.pdf
Institutional archive listing final title and in-press status: https://boa.unimib.it/handle/10281/628361
Final listed title: "On the welfare effects of compatibility with Hotelling competition: the role of market coverage." WP 2025 title lacked the subtitle.

- WP §2 derives a two-firm Hotelling differentiated-product price model with responsive FE network expectations, comparing complete, just-complete and uncovered markets.
- WP §3 explicitly finds higher prices and profits with compatibility, but whether market compatibility is socially excessive or insufficient depends crucially on coverage. Full coverage may yield **excessive** compatibility; uncovered-market participation gains can reverse the welfare conclusion.
- **P1-KS overlap:** FE prices with user-count network effects, compatibility effects on price/welfare, institutional importance of full market coverage.
- **Distinction:** no national standardization voting or cross-border ownership matrix is derived in this WP; P1-KS is fully covered with four asymmetric legacy-network firms.
- **Critical caveat:** the **2026 final author's manuscript is restricted**. All equations/details here refer to the accessible 2025 WP, not a verified identity with the forthcoming published version. This paper warns strongly against claiming that the P1-KS welfare result holds outside complete-market coverage.
