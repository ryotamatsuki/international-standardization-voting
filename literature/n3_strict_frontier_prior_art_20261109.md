## Round A. Committee coordination, adoption timing and experimentation
1. **Delaney & Driouchi (2025), Economic Theory 80:1177–1201**, *Coordination through Committees and Markets with Option Timing Games*. [PUBLISHER SUMMARY, original paper available as PDF]. An option timing game formally derives equilibrium strategies for consensus committees vs market-driven first adoption. Returns depend on unilateral-adoption and concession payoffs; equilibrium expected payoffs can be equal across coordination mechanisms. https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00199-025-01654-4 . Thus a committee-vs-market option-timing comparison cannot count as new.
2. **Ganglmair, Simcoe & Tarantino (2025), AEJ Microeconomics 17(3):164–190**, *Learning When to Quit*. [PUBLISHER SUMMARY]. IETF committee developers' continuation or abandonment under imperfect learning; dynamic discrete-choice estimation and subsidy vs publication prize welfare. https://www.aeaweb.org/articles?id=10.1257/mic.20190321 . Thus uncertainty, standard selection and endogenous abandonment are already modeled.
3. **Wang & Buccella (2023), Review of Industrial Organization**, *The Timing of Technology Adoption in Network Industries*. [INDEX SUMMARY]. Explicit leader and follower technology adoption under Cournot with network externalities, weakening innovation timing as a fresh idea. https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11151-023-09902-4 [FULL HTML, exact published paper now verified].
4. **Farrell & Simcoe (2012)** and **Simcoe (2012)** consensus-rule/delay baseline; cannot merely append committee delays.

**Screened-out directions:** “committee adoption versus early mover”, “stronger externalities hasten/slower standard migration”, and “publication prize reduces abandonments” are already in established models. No new N3 claim.

### Wang–Buccella exact-proposition check (added from publisher full HTML)
**Proposition 2 (2023)**: with rational expectations, the social planner's first adoption occurs earlier than market adoption; the second adoption timing's comparison changes with substitutability, and network strength affects the timing gap. **Proposition 4**: under output commitment, socially optimal first adoption is also earlier, but the network-intensity direction differs (first adoption accelerated in the planner problem; follower effects conditional on substitution). Paper also derives different rational-expectations vs output-commitment comparative statics for leader/follower. Therefore a generic \"network effects reverse the innovation timing gap\" would be directly pre-empted by this full-text theorem. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11151-023-09902-4

## Round B. Standardization, technology investment, patent incentives
5. **Foucart & Li (2021), Research Policy 50(2):104157**, *The Role of Technology Standards in Product Innovation: Theory and Evidence*. [PUBLISHER INTRO/THEORY DESCRIPTION]. Formally models standards as insurance for incremental vs radical innovation, with evidence on 1,143 UK firms over 2006–12. Predicts old standards foster incremental innovation and may slow new technology cycle, particularly low-efficiency/low-R&D firms. https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0048733320302316 . **Direct collision with earlier migration and latecomer innovation trap directions**. Original Proposition 1 identified by publisher, exact full theorem text still not audited here.
6. **Llanes (2024), IJIO 93:103046**, *Innovation Incentives in Technical Standards*. [PUBLISHER INTRO/HIGHLIGHTS]. Models developing complementary technologies for standards, ex ante price caps vs ex post patent pools, both insufficient and excessive innovation, different welfare rankings and cooperative R&D. https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0167718724000018 . Hence “SSO causes too little or too much complementary innovation” already theorem-level economics.
7. **Fiedler, Larrain & Prüfer (2023), Research Policy 52(6):104761**, *Membership, Governance and Lobbying in SSOs*. [PUBLISHER SUMMARY]. Fully endogenous innovator/implementer SSO membership for licensing, learning, implementation; implementer participation has inverted-U welfare/industry profit effects. https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0048733323000458 . Hence “more participation can make standard worse/better” is not newly original.
8. **Abe, Fukuda & Muto (2026), IJGT 55**, *Patent Pool Formation Between Rival Standards*. [PUBLISHER/INDEX SUMMARY]. Rival standards affect voluntary patent pool formation; existence of competing standard facilitates coalitions. https://doi.org/10.1007/s00182-026-00985-3
9. **Bergeaud, Schmidt & Zago (2026), Journal of Financial Economics 180:104281**, *Patents That Match Your Standards*. [PUBLISHER INTRO/ABSTRACT]. Near-standards patent holders gain sales/share early; competition raises R&D; late follower catch-up; temporary rents. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jfineco.2026.104281

**Screened-out directions:** innovator-vs-implementer membership effects, complementary investments and cross-generation catch-up are already explained at least qualitatively; changing the SSO institutional name cannot establish N3.

## Round C. Layered standards, product quality, developing-country technology capacity
10. **World Bank, WDR 2025 Standards for Development** [POLICY], officially calls for differentiated/tiered standards reflecting varying country capacities; standards can be modular or multiple stringency levels and should support technology progression. https://www.worldbank.org/en/publication/wdr2025 . This makes multilevel design economically relevant but **does not itself constitute a novel prediction**.
11. **Petropoulou (2013), Oxford Economic Papers 65(2):372–393**, *Vertical Product Differentiation, Minimum Quality Standards, and International Trade*. [PUBLISHER ABSTRACT]. Endogenous national quality thresholds, vertical differentiated duopoly in two markets; national thresholds inefficient, global-optimum standard can be unattainable by reciprocal agreement without transfer when quality-specialized. https://doi.org/10.1093/oep/gps023 .
12. **Gaigné & Larue (2016), American Journal of Agricultural Economics 98(5):1432–1449**, *Quality Standards, Industry Structure, and Welfare in a Global Economy*. [PUBLISHER ABSTRACT]. Heterogeneous productivity, public MQS and firms' own **higher** private standard chosen jointly with quality/price, industry selection and trade. https://doi.org/10.1093/ajae/aaw039 .
13. **Lutz (1996) CEPR DP1384**, *Trade Effects of Minimum Quality Standards with and without Deterred Entry*. [WP ABSTRACT]. Quality standards, trade, asymmetric entry deterrence; harmonization can select a single firm. https://cepr.org/publications/dp1384 .
14. **Macedoni & Weinberger (2024) CESifo WP11287**, *International Spillovers of Quality Regulations*. [WP ABSTRACT]. Differentiated productivity and variable markups; a quality regulation's fixed cost may improve terms of trade for other countries via high-quality entry. https://www.ifo.de/en/cesifo/publications/2024/working-paper/international-spillovers-quality-regulations .

**Screened-out directions:** offering multiple tiers, higher standards lowering low-productivity entry, and catch-up via standards are already policy concepts or modeled investment/trade effects. Exact **joint mechanism for endogenously selected tier architecture and downstream learning across country capability** might remain an economically important QUESTION, but its N3 independence cannot be established from the accessible source screens. DO NOT propose as a new proposition.

**Screened-out directions:** reference hub centrality and price effects, multihoming portfolios and vertical foreclosure are already represented by direct prior models. A proposed reference hub topology has no verified non-contained theorem or micro price/entry derivation.

## Potential false shortcuts caught
- World Bank's call for tiered rules is not evidence nobody theorized tiers.
- Dynamic standard choice and firm experimentation predate 2026; Delaney 2025 and Ganglmair 2025 are important overlooked close references.
- Endogenous innovator/implementer membership and complementary R&D were solved in reputable papers 2023/2024.
- “Network effects alter price” or “interface denial can protect a future entrant” is old and recently directly published.
- An arbitrary positive effect from a cross-term or forced discrete policy cannot be independent economic novelty.
- Do not conflate formal quality/safety standards with protocol/compatibility, ISO with IETF, or industry SSO consensus with a nation-state welfare-maximizing ballot.
