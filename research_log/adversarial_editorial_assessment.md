## 2. Five adversarial reviewers

### A. Industrial organization (IO)
- Strength H5: makes an actual supplier entry and screening choice the outcome rather than vote counts. Stronger if certification acts as fixed exporting cost shaping product market rivalry.
- Objection: an ability to replace one barrier by another is generic strategic trade policy, not standards economics. Must identify why technical harmonization changes *marginal policy return*, not postulate a convenient new tax.
- Strength H9: observed portfolios rather than mutually exclusive network affiliation. Objection: same standard + proprietary extension is traditional multi-homing/differentiation, not new.
- Verdict: H5/H9 research only; NO GO for newly constructed game.

### B. International economics
- H5 best aligned: technical standard convergence, duplicate tests and importer/exporter entry have international equilibrium content, unlike four-voter arithmetic. OECD 2026 and JIE empirical 2022 anchor policy relevance.
- Objection: imports respond to market-entry cost, and two entry barriers are already textbook. An N3 proposition must affect the **sign or new welfare/development ranking**, prove a boundary not known from Wallner/Costinot/Klimenko, and withstand asymmetric firm ownership tests.
- Verdict: H5 *worth priority search*, not yet a theory paper.

### C. Actual standard-setting/governance expert
- The ISO final national body ballot does not itself authorize product marketing. ISO standard text, mandatory country rules, national adoption, laboratory certificate, mutual-recognition agreement, and customs handling are independent operations.
- Government and accreditation-body MRAs have different legal power; OCA is an industry consortium, not ISO state votes; 3GPP member contributions are not national one-country-one-vote ISO FDIS ballots.
- Existing model should not conflate them. If this distinction is only verbal, the economic model adds nothing.
- Verdict: must choose ONE institution and show actual actors' discretion.

### D. Empirical IO/econometrics
- Identification is major bottleneck: technical harmonization and MRAs are negotiated in response to trade growth and lobbying. Sectoral HS–ICS links are many-to-many. OECD agreement documents do not give import inspection behavior.
- OCA optional flags yield a clean descriptive outcome but cannot estimate random EV-to-charger interoperability success, physical installed base, or an ISO country ballot.
- Require an observed firm/variety-entry treatment channel, credible untreated categories, and documentary evidence of enforcement.
- Verdict: H5 empirical feasibility conditional; H9 descriptive panel initially; H12 restricted micro-data.

### E. Journal editor
- A standalone mathematical cutoff showing a peak or U-shape is not enough. Existing 2026 publications now directly discuss strategic specification dilution, market-coverage ambiguity, cross-standard portfolios and patent-inclusion rents.
- Demand the final introduction: (i) one NEW economic prediction; (ii) closest THREE paper statements and why they cannot produce it; (iii) policy or market outcomes and credible falsifier; (iv) a minimum solvable mechanism.
- The 2026 OECD observation is important context but its publication does not establish N3 originality. Technical standards and conformity recognition *as separate variables* is not itself a theorem.
- **Editorial desk decision**: no newly discovered main theorem ready for a submission. Keep earlier JPET and ISO studies separate; move to a gated prior-art and data-feasibility stage.

## 3. Knockout tests for H5 before any game construction
K1. Read original specific results of Wallner 1998, Costinot 2008, Klimenko 2009 and any endogenous CAP model. If they already predict strategic substitution between harmonized standards and certification, **NO-GO**.
K2. Identify real product case with both converged technical texts and legally differentiated foreign certificate treatment, not merely ISO membership.
K3. Is there an **endogenous response** in the remaining recognition policy beyond mechanical residual costs? If no, published entry-cost gravity model suffices.
K4. If strategic choice comes from national welfare plus local profits, does the effect survive reasonable changes of market structure, country size, honest safety risk?
K5. Can one test the predicted differential pattern in trading partners, exporters' fixed costs, re-testing and entry? No credible outcome => RESEARCH or switch to more measurable question.
K6. Does ablation removing national testing-policy choice or foreign certificate regime change remove the distinctive qualitative result? If not, alleged novelty fails.
