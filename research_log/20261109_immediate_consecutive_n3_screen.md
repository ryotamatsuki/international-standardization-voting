## Track I: Deep trade integration, regulatory blocs and differentiated welfare
1. Parenti & Vannoorenberghe, **Journal of the European Economic Association (2025)**, A Simple Theory of Deep Trade Integration. Published abstract: international regulatory preferences and technology differences determine welfare-improving harmonization, mutual concessions and formation of regulatory blocs. Final publisher https://doi.org/10.1093/jeea/jvae046 . Accessible **OLDER 2019 PDF working version** https://respect.eui.eu/wp-content/uploads/sites/6/2019/12/Parenti_RESPECT-1.pdf : Proposition 1 technology differences necessary not sufficient for trade; Proposition 2 import occurs for bounded interval of foreign regulatory preferences; Proposition 4 log-supermodularity of bilateral trade in foreign/domestic regulatory preferences; Proposition 5 multiple optimal producing standard sources with heterogeneous preferences; Proposition 6 optimal assignment interval/no-hole property. **Important caveat:** exact 2019 paper propositions cannot be imputed to the final 2025 journal pagination or numbering without comparing final article.
2. Takarada & Kawabata (2025), Nagoya City University Discussion Paper 701, https://oikono.econ.nagoya-cu.ac.jp/wp-content/uploads/sites/2/2025/02/DP701_Kawabata_February2025.pdf : Original explicit propositions viewed. Proposition 1: under *national* member welfare regional recognition harms its members in specified ranges; nonmember effects depend on spillover parameters. Proposition 2: under *joint* member welfare regional mutual recognition weakly benefits all, potentially strictly. Proposition 3: multilateral MR may benefit regional members while harming former nonmembers. **Crucial institutional distinction in original p2 footnote 3:** mutual recognition of the *product standards* is different from mutual recognition of *conformity assessment test results*. This paper cannot simply be represented as a CAP theorem.
3. Maggi & Mrázová, Harmonization... What Else? The Role for International Regulatory Agreements, CEPR DP19840 (2025) / NBER WP33318 (2024) https://cepr.org/publications/dp19840 . They show spontaneous harmonization can be inefficient and an agreement can favor regulatory diversity. Thus broad “more harmonization can be bad” is old. Exact updated full proposition comparison remains necessary.
Decision: generic strategic regulatory blocs/outsider harmed, voluntarily coordinated standards, or preference-induced trade reversal **NO-GO**.

## Formally checked necessary baseline (not an N3 theorem)
Let exporters' productivity phi have Pareto survival probability P(phi>=x)=(phi_min/x)^k, and market profit before destination fixed entry cost F_ij is D_ij phi^{sigma−1}, sigma>1. Under an interior export cutoff
phi*_ij=(F_ij/D_ij)^{1/(sigma−1)}, 
S_ij=(D_ij phi_min^{sigma−1}/F_ij)^{k/(sigma−1)}.
Hence d log S_ij/d log F_ij=−k/(sigma−1). A bilateral Phase-I/II activation that ONLY reduces F_ij raises participation in that specific market, but **cannot increase third-country k participation unless the model explicitly contains a credible cross-market input (shared accredited capacity, sunk infrastructure, supplier-network sourcing, regulatory trust or information flow)**; in the separable base case dS_ik/dF_ij=0 for distinct j,k. This is an analytic separability tautology/Melitz-like result, **not a publishable or original theorem**.
