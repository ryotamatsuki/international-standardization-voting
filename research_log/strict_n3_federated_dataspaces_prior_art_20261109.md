## Why this is a different search direction
Previous ISO-standardization hypotheses around country voting, conformance adoption, optional scope, common reference implementations, generational innovation and tiered quality standards directly collide with existing published theoretical results. This pass asks whether *a technical interoperability standard combined with decentralized cross-jurisdiction data governance and sovereign trust* creates a qualitatively distinct market-entry, innovation or cross-border-welfare prediction. This is a test of research relevance, not an assertion of originality.

## New 2026 primary real-world evidence
- OECD (2026-10-20), *Data Spaces between Co-ordination Challenges, Financial Viability and Public Policy*, DOI https://doi.org/10.1787/0066f11b-en . Study reviewed 235 IDSA Radar initiatives in February 2026; only 25 operational, none at designated scaling stage. Four boundary conditions for federated infrastructure advantage: 3+ participants, inability to trust single intermediary, non-excludable collective value, continuous governance.
- OECD distinguishes standards-based common protocol from governability/identity, regulatory-driven onboarding, anchor participant, public funding and cross-border implementation. Data ecosystems need real use-case value and trusted sharing. EU Catena-X and Japan Ouranos proof-of-concept exchanges battery carbon footprint data across jurisdictions. A pilot is NOT commercial equilibrium evidence.
- OECD report points to ISO/IEC DIS 20151-1 (concepts), DIS 26450 (DSP), DIS 26451 (DCP) and CEN/CENELEC 2026 work as actual technical normalization process, not an existing fully approved ISO standard. Source https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/data-spaces-between-co-ordination-challenges-financial-viability-and-public-policy_0066f11b-en/full-report/understanding-data-spaces_e3dfceed.html
- IPA Open Data Spaces RAM v2 (2026-04-01), source https://www.ipa.go.jp/digital/architecture/reports/ouranos-ecosystem-dataspaces-ram-white-paper.html ; Japanese public implementation context. Distinguish from national-government ISO ballot.
- OECD (2026) policy says public grants without use-case revenue can perpetuate non-viable technical infrastructure; this is already known economic intuition of public support and demand.

## Direct economic and other theory collisions discovered
1. **Rhodes, Jidong Zhou & Junjie Zhou (2026), "Digital Ecosystems and Data Regulation", Yale Cowles DP 2426R1** https://cowles.yale.edu/research/cfdp-2426r1-digital-ecosystems-and-data-regulation . Explicit price and innovation competition between multi-product ecosystem and single-product providers; forced data sharing/cooperative can HARM single-product firms by triggering incumbent aggressive pricing; compensation can rescue consumer welfare. Therefore "common data-sharing standard raises data-sharing but worsens entry" is NOT independently novel as an economic direction. Access confirmed abstract, not all full proofs.
2. **Kretschmer, Rasch, Shekar & Wenzel (2025), CEPR DP20383, "Mandating Privacy Spillovers: Interoperability in Data Markets"** https://cepr.org/publications/dp20383 . Mandated interoperability can amplify data/privacy spillovers and harm consumer welfare and entrant market share; incumbent free data model vs privacy-oriented entrant. Thus reduced contestability with data interoperability is already modeled; no simple new N3 hypothesis here. This is a WP and exact proof exclusion not completed.
3. **Liu, Wang & Xu (2026), International Review of Economics & Finance 110, 105619, "Supply chain data sharing in the era of trusted data spaces: A four-party evolutionary game analysis"** https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S105905602600732X . Direct game-theoretic multi-party trusted data spaces with heterogeneity and coordination costs (publisher abstract accessible; full text access blocked). Mere 4-party data-space adoption incentives is thus not new.
4. **Verstraete et al (2025), ITS conference paper, "Data Spaces Business Models: Unravelling Individual Actors' Incentives"** https://www.econstor.eu/handle/10419/331315 . Observes co-creation, network effects, participant benefits and costs for several data spaces; not peer reviewed formal theorem, but institution and adoption incentives are established observations.
5. **Soininen et al (2026), Data in Brief, "Interoperability Architecture for Data Spaces"** https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2352340926002313 . Published technical approach to federating different data spaces; economic game unproven but technical interface federation by itself not a discovery.
6. **World Bank (2025), WDR *Standards for Development*** https://www.worldbank.org/en/publication/wdr2025 . Calls for tiered standards, shared quality infrastructure, international cooperation; existing economics of heterogeneous quality standards and development already near the associated economic story.
