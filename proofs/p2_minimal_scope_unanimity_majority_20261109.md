# P2 Gate — endogenous compatibility scope: unanimity vs 3-of-4 (2026-11-09)

**Independent result status:** explicit **GLOBAL price Nash equilibrium existence** proof via 896 exact sufficient geometric inequalities, for all 16 standard-adoption profiles, at \(d=1/3,\ x\in[0,1]\); complete **finite adoption-stage trembling-hand perfect equilibrium selection** in the published-scope model for \(x>0\), conditional on these selected price continuations; exact national payoff and agenda-setter result. This is **not** a claim of uniqueness of all price Nash equilibria nor a verified new-to-literature theorem. **CRUCIAL:** ISO committee "consensus" is not unanimity; q=4 is a counterfactual strict-veto rule, q=3 a simplified four-valid-vote approval rule.

## 1. Keep the original product market but endogenize one linkage

Four national owners and four firms equally spaced at 0,1,2,3 on a length-four Salop circle, squared transport cost, four independent national destination markets, pricing discrimination allowed, fully covered, zero marginal cost. Four country population shares \(w_i>0\) sum to one, \(N=1\) unless otherwise specified.

Initially firms 0–2 all share incumbent standard L, firm3 uses incompatible M, so \(O=1110\). A new proposed gateway/standard offers interoperability strength \(x\in[0,1]\) **between entrant3 and each compatible incumbent**; the incumbents' pre-existing mutual links stay at strength 1. For the all-join profile F, network-quality bonuses are
\[
a_0=a_1=a_2=d(2+x),\qquad a_3=3dx.
\]
For *arbitrary* adoption profile \(s\), let \(m=s_0+s_1+s_2\); then for incumbent \(i\le2\), \(a_i=d s_i(m-1+x s_3)\), and \(a_3=d x m s_3\). This is a **weighted-brand-compatibility** interpretation, NOT the endogenously fulfilled *user-count* Katz–Shapiro network model. It keeps original Gandal–Shy style quadratic price competition tractable.

Stage 0 country 1 (a national agenda-setter, not an undefined benevolent maximizer) proposes x. Stage 1 the four national bodies simultaneously cast valid Yes/No ballots; if x is approved, stage 2 firms voluntarily join a publicly documented gateway, and stage 3 firms set prices. If rejected, the historic O state remains (assuming privately implementing the *unpublished* interface is not viable due to high costs). Firm3's resource implementation expense if it joins approved x is \(\kappa x^2\) with \(\kappa=1/100\). All incumbent firms choose L with zero switching fees; their outside options are distinct \(X_i\). Approval enables the low-cost new implementation **exogenously** as in P1, not automatically via ISO rules.

**New and economically consequential cost primitive:** country 0 also suffers an **unavoidable real complement/legacy-infrastructure adjustment burden** \(c_0x^2\) when the new interoperability is deployed. The burden is external to the four firms' private adoption payoffs and is allocated to country0 national welfare, even if country0 casts No. Its economic/institutional microfoundation remains open. **It is NOT a mandatory ISO standards adoption fee; the standards remain formally voluntary.** Its unavoidable nature needs external-effect or legacy infrastructure explanation to avoid inconsistency with opting out. Do **not** disguise this as generated by Salop pricing.

## 2. Pricing, consumer surplus, industry profit (derived from firm optimizing)

For all-incumbents-plus-entrant adopter profile \(F_x\) in the locally adjacent price game,
\[
p_0=p_2=1+\frac{d(1-x)}3,\quad
p_1=1+\frac{d(1-x)}6,\quad
p_3=1-\frac{5d(1-x)}6.
\]
Unit-circle demand density \(q_i=p_i\). For \(x=0\), these equal the old O prices; for \(x=1\), all prices are 1. Worldwide operating profit is \(p_i^2/4\). Relative to old O:
\[
\begin{aligned}
\Delta C(x)&=\frac{dx(216-26d+13dx)}{36},\\
\Delta\Pi_0=\Delta\Pi_2&=\frac{dx(dx-2d-6)}{36},\\
\Delta\Pi_1&=\frac{dx(dx-2d-12)}{144},\\
G_3(x)=\Delta\Pi_3&=\frac{5dx(5dx-10d+12)}{144},\\
\Delta W^{gross}_{world}(x)&=\frac{dx(216-94d+47dx)}{144}.
\end{aligned}
\]
For nation i, national gain relative to rejection O is
\[
\Delta W_i(x)=w_i\Delta C(x)/4+\Delta\Pi_i(x)
-\mathbf 1_{i=3}\kappa x^2-\mathbf1_{i=0}c_0x^2.
\]

## 3. No-go theorem: no overcompatibility when the only real investment cost is voluntarily borne by entrant

Assume \(c_0=0\) and no other external harmonization costs; firm 3 voluntarily implements approved \(x>0\) only if
\(G_3(x)\ge\kappa x^2\). This means
\[
\kappa x\le\frac{5d(12-10d)+25d^2x}{144}.
\]
The world net welfare function is
\[
W(x)=\frac{d(216-94d)}{144}x+
\left(\frac{47d^2}{144}-\kappa\right)x^2.
\]
At every implementable \(x>0\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
W'(x)&=
\frac{d(216-94d)}{144}
+2\left(\frac{47d^2}{144}-\kappa\right)x\\
&\ge \frac{d(216-94d)-10d(12-10d)+44d^2x}{144}\\
&=\boxed{\frac{d(96+6d+44dx)}{144}>0}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Thus overcompatibility relative to the socially efficient *privately implementable* scope cannot arise **from a sole entrant's voluntary quadratic joining cost** in this particular market. This is a non-vacuous adoption-discipline bound. For each incumbent owner \(i\), \(\Delta W_i(x)/x\) has a strictly positive slope in x without country-specific adjustment costs; therefore a reluctant existing-network country cannot be induced to support *only* low/intermediate x under the simple model. A meaningful unanimity compromise ceiling needs another mechanism.

**Important:** This no-go is conditional on the exact weighted-brand network model, interior pricing, no uncompensated costs and no congested networks; does not generalize automatically to KS current-user FE models. Newness versus existing theory unverified.

## 4. Constructive counterexample with one national cost: under > optimum > over in scope

Take
\[
\boxed{d=1/3,\quad
w=(11/50,2/5,7/50,6/25),\quad
\kappa=1/100,\quad c_0=3/10.}
\]
The national gain polynomials are EXACT:
\[
\begin{aligned}
\Delta W_0(x)&=\frac{x(2842-19097x)}{64800},\\
\Delta W_1(x)&=\frac{31x(x+34)}{6480},\\
\Delta W_2(x)&=\frac{x(97x+118)}{21600},\\
\Delta W_3(x)&=\frac{x(379x+6982)}{32400}.
\end{aligned}
\]

For every x>0, country1 (the proposer) strictly prefers larger x, and countries1,2,3 strictly support implementation. Country0 supports iff \(0<x\le x_C\) with \(x_C=2842/19097\); assume an indifferent voter votes Yes.

If the strict counterfactual **unanimity** rule q=4 applies, country1 selects the **maximum acceptable** \(x^U=x_C\). Under the simplified 3-of-4 rule q=3, country1 selects \(x^Q=1\), and countries1,2,3 vote Yes. With real infrastructure plus private entrant costs, world net gain is
\[
\boxed{W(x)=\frac{x(13850-8869x)}{32400}}.
\]
Its unique unconstrained maximizer is \(x^W=6925/8869\). Consequently,
\[
\boxed{
x^U=\frac{2842}{19097}\approx0.148819
< x^W=\frac{6925}{8869}\approx0.780810
< x^Q=1.
}
\]
World net gains: W(xU)=0.05755316835; W(xW)=0.16688599422; W(1)=0.15373456790. **Although q=3 over-standardizes relative to W optimum, its world welfare is HIGHER than q=4 in this particular calibration.**

Second example changes just c0 to 1/2, keeping all other primitives and strategies:
\(x^U=2842/32057\approx0.0886545840\);
\(x^W=6925/15349\approx0.4511694573\);
\(x^Q=1\).
Net world welfare:
W(xU)=0.03417371750, W(xW)=0.09643050900, W(1)=-0.04626543210. Here q=3 yields **strictly lower world welfare than strict unanimity** and is globally inefficient vs rejection, despite all four firms' scope-specific continuation and voluntary entrant adoption.

Since every relevant inequality is strict except the deliberate country0 participation equality at xU, by continuity these results persist under small perturbations of the economic primitives, with the endogenous participation cutoff adjusted continuously. These are open-set **conditional model results**, not proven broad social regularities.

## 5. Pricing/adoption and global best-response verification

For d=1/3 and **every x in [0,1]** the uploaded program checks **all sixteen profiles** s and 4 firms per profile, 14 exact strict geometric sufficient inequalities per firm (896 in total). Each inequality is affine in x, so checking exact endpoints proves interval-wide positivity.

Let \(b_j=p^*_j-a_j\), define the local right/left zero-price reach:
\(R_i(0)=(1+b_{i+1}+a_i)/2\), \(L_i(0)=(1+b_{i-1}+a_i)/2\). Verified conditions: \(p_i^*>0\), equilibrium next-neighbor distances \(0<R_i(p_i^*),L_i(p_i^*)<1\), zero-price reaches \(0<R_i(0),L_i(0)<3/2\), \(|R_i(0)-L_i(0)|<1\), opposite firm undercuts the deviator beyond ±3/2, and the opposite firm does **not** undercut at selected local consumer boundaries. These imply for every unilateral nonnegative price z, \(q_i(z)\le\max(0,2p_i^*-z)\). The inequality \(zq_i(z)\le(p_i^*)^2\) establishes a **global pure Nash best response** for the proposed price vector in *each* subgame. This proves price equilibrium **existence**, not uniqueness of all possible equilibria.

For each x>0, the incumbent's L choice weakly dominates private X_i: 21 unilateral state comparisons strictly favor L and 3 are indifferent. Entrant's joining incremental payoff when all incumbents are L is, with d=1/3 and kappa=1/100,
\[
G_3(x)-\kappa x^2=\frac{x(301x+3250)}{32400}>0.
\]
Hence F_x is a strict pure adoption equilibrium on approval, and the weakly dominated all-singleton equilibrium is eliminated by trembling-hand perfection of the *finite* adoption stage. Rejection selects O given no viable unpublished adapter. This does not prove unique SPNE of the entire continuous extensive game.

## 6. Editorial novelty and institutional constraints

- ISO/IEC Directives Part 1 (2026): technical committee consensus is NOT unanimity, and the committee-chair absence-of-sustained-opposition rule is NOT identical to an actual country veto. q=4 must be named a hypothetical unanimity benchmark, not 'ISO consensus'; q=3 with all four countries casting valid Yes/No ballots is an *idealized restricted full ballot*.
- Goerke & Holler (1995), *Public Choice*, already show sensitivity of standards' welfare consequences to majority-rule manipulation in a network economy. Hence "unanimity under, majority over" is not intrinsically novel.
- Farrell & Simcoe (2012), *RAND Journal of Economics*, compare standardization consensus rules with information, no-compromise bargaining and delay; this model differs in endogenous scope, public information and market prices, but that difference is not sufficient for novelty.
- Spulber (2019), *Economic Journal*, analyzes market competition and SSO voting and proves efficiency in certain configurations. Our example changes the national distribution of real complementary infrastructure costs and proposal-stage objectives, so it is not a logical contradiction.
- **Main weakness:** \(c_0x^2\) is an additional **unavoidable** national expense **outside firms' voluntary optimization**, precisely the type of assumption that can manufacture the directional welfare comparison. Without empirically and game-theoretically credible grounding, this is a respectable *illustration*, not automatically a journal-worthy new theorem.
- **Another weakness:** country1 is explicitly selected as the agenda setter and prefers to maximize x in the example; that is endogenous to its national payoffs, but exogenous allocation of proposal rights. The result may collapse under a welfare-maximizing agenda-setter or fully endogenous bargaining.
- Voluntary enterprise adoption is strict in every approved x>0, so it does **not create a separate adoption-voting conflict in this example**. The same choice of scope would arise under automatic adoption. This makes the novel ISO-specific added value small.
- **Next research gate** only if warranted: introduce the *smallest defensible microfoundation for a country's external burden* or a model where the *voluntary adoption constraint binds endogenously and changes the supported scope*, and explicitly check against Goerke & Holler, Farrell & Simcoe, and Spulber. Otherwise stop P2 exploration and prioritize a well-focused publication from already secured results.
