# Final shortlist: candidate propositions, not proved theorems
As of 2026-11-09 JST. **Executive scientific verdict: RESEARCH, no N3/N4-certified main proposition**. The most useful improvement to the earlier three-round search is shifting attention from the *breadth of standards specification* (already directly collided with Ott et al. 2026) to a **separately governed downstream conformity-assessment recognition instrument** documented by OECD on 2026-11-05. This is a fresh question, NOT a claim of first-in-the-literature.

## 1. Preferred H5 — standard harmonization may be offset by national conformity-assessment choices
### Economically interpretable proposition to test
**If international harmonization narrows countries' ability to discriminate through technical product specifications but leaves foreign laboratory/certificate recognition discretionary, national actors with locally concentrated incumbent producer rents may respond by tightening or withholding recognition of foreign conformity-assessment results; accordingly the realized expansion of foreign exporter entry may fall short of the predicted harmonization gain, and simultaneous technical harmonization plus certificate mutual recognition could have a non-additive trade effect.** Every directional statement is a research hypothesis, not a theorem or verified behavioral fact.

### Why it would matter
Existing theories often model standard differences as unit or fixed entry costs; an actual importing firm must also **prove conformity**. This raises an independently manipulable non-tariff-margin question: after one trade barrier is removed, does the other expand strategically, remain fixed, or contract because test compatibility improves? Theory could distinguish *policy substitution*, *fixed-technology complements*, and *benign learning/trust*. If it predicts a clear institutional test, it changes recommendations from harmonizing technical texts alone to joint governance of document and verification instruments. This is distinct from a declaration that published ISO standards automatically reduce costs.

### Closest literature and novelty danger (three-plus)
- Gandal & Shy (2001): national recognition and domestic profits, but foreign standard recognition rather than foreign conformity certificate acceptance. DOI 10.1016/S0022-1996(00)00067-2.
- Costinot (2008): NT/MR institutions and inefficient standard policies. DOI 10.1016/j.jinteco.2007.11.004.
- Klimenko (2009): strategically distorted national compatibility choices, DOI 10.1016/j.jinteco.2008.08.005.
- Wallner (1998 WP) already uses product standards as strategic trade tools; generic barrier substitution may be straightforward: https://ebslgwp.hhs.se/hastef/abs/hastef0254.htm .
- Schmidt & Steingress (2022): standard harmonization, endogenous firm adoption and trade; OECD 2016 theoretical framework explicitly distinguishes specification, conformity and information costs. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jinteco.2022.103619 and https://doi.org/10.1787/3fbf60b1-en.
- OECD (2026-11-05): *observes* legal-operational gaps, does not prove the strategic response: https://doi.org/10.1787/56f2cfc7-en.

### Mechanism, policy value, testable predictions
The importer faces two separable entry margins: specification redesign and local confirmation/testing. Harmonization removes first margin; national incentives may shift effort into second. Prediction A (only conditional): technical harmonization accompanied by no accepted foreign conformity reports yields smaller entrant and trade response than same specification convergence with operational test-report recognition. Prediction B (distinctive but more vulnerable): in settings where incumbent protection previously took place through technical specifications, post-harmonization testing barriers grow relative to comparable less-protected sectors. This second prediction is especially important: if not established by equilibrium primitives rather than postulated behavior, discard the project. Falsifiers: no difference in duplicated testing; similar trade-entry margins after harmonization with and without MRA; increase is entirely safety/quality justified and not concentrated in rent-exposed sectors.

### Minimum economic structure to assess LATER (not built here)
Existing heterogeneous exporter model with two well-defined compliance channels; a national actor choosing *one* remaining conformity recognition instrument; national benefit from consumers plus locally owned producer rents; exporter participation and trade quantities. Only after validating H5's independence should there be a standards choice; no ISO voting stage required unless it generates a unique result. A reduced-form second compliance cost **alone** is not publishable novelty.

### Fatal problems
Standards vs conformity recognition is a familiar two-NTB structure. An endogenous policy instrument can trivially replace another trade restriction, giving a generic taxes-and-standards result. Legal obligation under MRAs and genuine quality/safety screening restrict discretion. Micro-data matching technical documents, CAB recognition and importer-level entry is difficult. The outcome may have **N2** originality, not N3. Gate **RESEARCH**.

## 2. Secondary H9 — multi-standard platform investment after harmonization
### Candidate
Where products use multiple third-party and proprietary interfaces, stronger standard harmonization could increase the private return to proprietary extensions for complement access, potentially yielding more standards use but no equivalent increase in *cross-vendor* access. Not necessarily so; technology composition and bottleneck control determine sign.
### Why potentially significant
Single-standard-versus-fragmentation binary models miss partial, layered portfolios. Distinguish compatibility among users, complementary devices and platform owners, instead of simply “firm adopts or not.” Scope may influence entry and price competition, not just network size.
### Predecessors
Katz–Shapiro (1985); Economides–Flyer (1998); Berends–Deken–Hummel (2026, 97 smart-home platforms; empirical platform-mode propositions, not mathematical theorem); Bianchi–Martimort–Straub WP (rev. 2026); Farrell–Saloner installed-base literature.
### Empirical and falsification
Smart-home interface/protocol support and product/device connectivity, number of supported complements, entry and pricing. Prediction falsified if platform vendors' proprietary interface support monotonically declines following independent standard coverage improvements or any change reduces simply to old lock-in model. Need new strategic cross-layer bargaining or exclusivity **only if institutionally justified**. The standard coexistence itself is proven empirically, not an original theorem. **N2/U, RESEARCH**.

## 3. Secondary H12 — fragmented disclosure and aggregate technology-quality selection
### Candidate
When technology contributions in international SSO are complements, targeted technology disclosure restrictions can lower the quality and later adoption of agreed interfaces even in cases where immediately observed committee proposal approval influence rebalances toward a sanctioned competitor. Quality/adoption direction is conditional on knowledge substitutability and institutional access.
### Closest studies
Chen & Xiao (2026 JIBS, empirical 3GPP sanctions); Farrell & Simcoe (2012, consensus rule/delay); Spulber (2019, inventors, vote and SEP royalties); Rosero et al (2026, complement provision); Bergeaud et al (2026, standard-related innovation).
### Observable and difficulty
Approved technical contributions, independent feature maturity, standard implementer certification and cross-platform test outcomes, product innovations. Need SSO proprietary proposal and firm-level data; existing Chen–Xiao already identifies backfire in voting influence and technological complementarity, making original signal hard. **N2/U, RESEARCH**.

## 4. Comparison to previous in-repo finalists
- The existing R5 model (formal scope s vs optional-feature adoption a) derives I=s*a² hump, but survives theta=0 and deleting governance. It cannot be the headline of an ISO/network international economics paper. Ott et al 2026 also explicitly identify adversarial standard dilution.
- The previously constructed R12 backward-compatibility/upgrade model produces a conditional U-shaped innovation response; prior innovation and compatibility investment literature already contains these incentives. Not a ready replacement.
- The rejected JPET Cournot/design manuscript and this ISO/Salop project are distinct. Its X captures residual similarity, not actual technical interoperability H, and should not be relabeled to support candidate H5/H9 without rederivation.

## 5. Ordered gates
**G0 literature**: read original full theorem/working-paper sections closest to H5 (Wallner, Costinot, Klimenko, Schmidt–Steingress and technical verification policy papers); seek work on endogenous conformity assessment testing protection. If generic substitution is fully established -> NO-GO.
**G1 institutional**: identify a specific product category where both technical harmonization and distinct CAB mutual recognition timing are public, and where recognition really lowers duplicate tests.
**G2 strategic**: show a protective testing change not explained by safety regime or measurement artifacts, or derive a nontrivial complementarity/inefficiency condition with economic primitives.
**G3 theory**: only then formulate minimal game, prove open parameter-region result, ablate choices and state a sharp prediction in trade-entry or welfare.
**G4 submission**: target journal once published-theorem noncontainment, robust competitive continuation and external validity are demonstrated. No publication probability stated.

## Final decision
No selected proposition meets all three questions: “what genuinely new general fact?”, “which observed behavior better explained?”, and “not implied by an existing theorem?” **RESEARCH** for H5, H9 and H12; **NO-GO** for recycling previously solved inversions. GO for a new formal theorem is **not granted**.
