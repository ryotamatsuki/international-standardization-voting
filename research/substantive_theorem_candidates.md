## Priority 1 — OPTIONAL certified interface profiles and effective interoperation
**Problem:** A standard may be internationally published and core compliance certified while firms leave technically important optional profiles unsupported; the probability that a randomly paired cross-vendor system supports the required service is not captured by certificate/Core counts. A broader profile has an adoption-margin opportunity cost.

**Result found:** Full exact micro-founded cutoff model shows higher formal breadth s can lower realized pairwise optional interoperability I=s a^2 after a scope threshold. A nonparametric cutoff-density condition generalizes the sign. The math and boundaries are in proofs/new_main_theorem.md. All exact tests pass.

**Scientific limitation:** Private adoption cost vs network returns has been studied for decades; the paradox survives theta=0 and the absence of committee voting. This is likely a formal application of established selection/compliance economics, N2 until demonstrably more is added. Do not promote it to a paper by forcing an arbitrary political preference or harmonization fee.

**Live field evidence:** OCPP 2.0.1 Core certification mandatory, advanced security, smart charging, ISO15118 support optional. OCA lists product organization, version, date and optional flags publicly. Published ISO 15118-23 explicitly distinguishes conformance tests from pairwise interoperability, and CEC VOLTS documents actual cross-vendor test events. No raw paired pass/fail records verified.

**What would upgrade to N3:** prove a joint *national institutional choice × endogenous strategic vendor implementation* response that cannot be reproduced with simple selection and that predicts adoption, realized compatibility and either trade/entry or cross-border market access. Must survive direct Zhao–Spulber–Boom/GMS comparisons and a market-competition continuation.

## Priority 2 — standards that are used *in combination*, not binary alternatives
**Problem:** Smart-home/EV/payment firms commonly combine third-party open interface requirements with proprietary extensions or multiple standards; compatibility across versions may be incomplete. **Evidence:** Research Policy (2026), longitudinal 97 smart-home platforms, documents dual sourcing of interfaces and complementors. OCA distinguishes OCPP1.6 (incompatible with OCPP2.0.1), OCPP2.1 backward-compatible with 2.0.1. Some standards optional profile configurations are not necessarily binary.
