# Three independent searches and candidate propositions (HYPOTHESES ONLY)
Date: 2026-11-09. No new payoff function, demand function, game stages or equilibrium construction. The “would” signs are predictions to test, not proved statements.

## Round 1 — historical theory limits: candidates 1–4
**H1 Network-driven national vote reversal.** [Condition] asymmetric domestic installed bases; [change] higher network intensity; [outcome] support for welfare-improving international standard declines, through protection of incumbent networks. Prior art Barrett–Yang 2001, Klimenko 2009, Cheng–Huang 2025. Empirics country ballots unavailable. **N1/NO-GO**.
**H2 Majority rule worsens international harmonization.** With different consumer/producer rents, relaxing ISO voting quota can select a welfare-inferior standard even as a previously blocked beneficial standard enters; vote-versus-surplus misalignment. Goerke–Holler 1995, Spulber 2019, Gandal–Shy 2001. Ballot data unavailable; quorum arithmetic insufficient. **N1–N2/NO-GO**.
**H3 More national-standard publication but lower firm take-up.** When adaptation costs increase, publishing a common standard may leave more vendors on legacy protocols; classic voluntary implementation and switching costs. Zhao–Xia–Shaw 2007, Barrett–Yang 2001, Schmidt–Steingress 2022. Certification shares testable. **N1–N2/NO-GO headline**.
**H4 Product redesign after standard selection changes national voting.** When firms adjust residual characteristics, national support may change through price and rents. Boom 2001, Ruiz 2004 WP, GMS 2021, existing JPET. Quality/product differentiation observable with difficulty; mechanism known. **N2/NO-GO**.

## Round 2 — observed institutional/commercial discrepancies: candidates 5–8
**H5 Technical-text harmonization and strategic certification substitution [PREFERRED].** When states converge to a shared technical specification, but keep separate control over whether foreign test results are recognized, the incentive of some importing-country stakeholders to preserve duplicative domestic testing may rise (or fall) as standards-related discriminatory tools disappear; thus harmonization alone may have a smaller trade-entry gain than harmonization plus mutual recognition. Mechanism: strategic substitution/complementarity of specification and conformity-policy instruments. Closest: Wallner 1998 WP, Costinot 2008, Klimenko 2009, Schmidt–Steingress 2022, WTO/OECD 2016/2026. **Sign NOT verified and likely old under generic trade-barrier substitution; N2/U, RESEARCH**. Test: firm entry/export and foreign certificate acceptance after staggered document harmonization/MRA; false if no differential testing/exits.
**H6 Standard scope and optional certification.** When higher technical requirements raise heterogeneous fixed costs and network gains do not compensate, more ambitious certified functionality can lower the probability that two randomly matched products implement common optional functions. Mechanism already in Zhao 2007 and Ott et al 2026 (adversarial scope) plus older costly adoption; repo derived hump without governance. OCA optional feature flags observable; pairwise success not verified. **N2/NO-GO as headline**.
**H7 Certification mutual recognition without implementation in customs operations.** With MRA but no interoperable customs acceptance process, legal recognition may not materially lower realized delivery time or market access. Institutionally important, documented by OECD 2026; largely a workflow frictions observation, not economically new. Test border clearance; accessible data uncertain. **N1–N2/NO-GO as standalone theory**.
**H8 Generation-dependent compatibility investment.** When version conversion supports old users but exposes new products to cannibalization, longer mandated backward support may shift firm investment away from frontier generation in some conditions but toward upgrades in others. Farrell–Saloner 1986, Regibeau–Rockett 1996, Kretschmer–Claussen 2016, Cox et al 2023. OCPP versions observable. **N2/U RESEARCH only with new institutional commitment interaction**.

## Round 3 — competing predictions/market-structure boundaries: candidates 9–12
**H9 Multistandard portfolios may defeat uniform technical convergence [SECOND].** When platform providers can bridge a shared third-party interface AND privately extend one component, a standard harmonization may increase proprietary extension uptake despite increasing nominal open-standard participation; entrant access may increase or decline depending on complement rights and switching costs. Mechanism strategic two-level differentiation/cross-standard access. Berends et al 2026, Katz–Shapiro 1985, Economides–Flyer 1998, Bianchi–Martimort–Straub 2026 WP. Test protocol portfolios, user access, firm entry. **N2/U RESEARCH**. Sign conjectural.
**H10 Standardization induces asymmetric temporary innovation rents.** Technological leaders gain first, then followers catch up, potentially changing direction of R&D. Bergeaud et al 2026 JFE, Spulber 2019, Bonani 2026 IJIO. Patent alignment/revenue observable; economic fact already supported. **N1–N2/NO-GO**.
**H11 Market coverage reverses optimal interoperability policy.** If platform coverage initially incomplete, network inclusion gains may dominate pricing competition; under near-full coverage market-power effects dominate. Bertoletti 2026, Bianchi et al WP 2026, Katz–Shapiro 1985. Coverage data possible; sign well-known. **N1–N2/NO-GO**.
**H12 Cross-border technical restrictions change joint complement provision [THIRD].** When international SSO participants hold mutually complementary technical knowledge, restricting disclosure may weaken both quality of selected interfaces and vendors' willingness to implement them, changing trade and R&D beyond documented voting influence. Chen–Xiao 2026, Farrell–Simcoe 2012, Spulber 2019, Rosero et al 2026. Proposal approvals observable in studies; actual welfare and software conformance access unclear. **N2/U RESEARCH**.

## Cross-round elimination
Historical candidate 1–4 fail independently; observed candidate 6–7 fail since standards complexity/operational recognition already discussed; candidate 8 requires known transition economics; theory-conflict candidate 10–11 already explained. Shortlist: H5, H9, H12; H8 as monitor only. No certified N3. The criterion is a **new economically meaningful conditional comparative static absent closest economic mechanisms**, NOT a different four-country Salop specification.

## What would refute the preferred H5
(1) Conformity assessment under national law is fixed by technical safety objective, so harmonization does NOT change its strategically chosen stringency; (2) certificate-recognition MRAs directly remove re-testing regardless of standard choice; (3) under the most natural oligopoly/entry structure, standard harmonization and conformance rules are separable and no substitution response exists; (4) generic trade instrument substitution already completely characterizes the outcome. Any of these supports NO-GO.
